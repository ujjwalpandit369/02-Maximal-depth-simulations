Anatomy of a Black Hole Spacetime

The Schwarzschild Solution & Kruskal-Szekeres Coordinates

The Schwarzschild metric is the simplest solution to Einstein's equations for a black hole. However, it has a "coordinate singularity" at the event horizon (r=2M), which is just an artifact of the chosen coordinate system. By transforming to Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates, we can remove this artifact and reveal the true, maximal geometry of the spacetime, which is what the Penrose diagram shows.

The Four Regions

Region I (Our Universe): The asymptotically flat region where we can exist safely. Region II (Black Hole): The interior of the black hole. Once an object crosses the event horizon to enter this region, it is forced to move towards the singularity at r=0. Region III (Parallel Universe): Another asymptotically flat region, causally disconnected from our own. Region IV (White Hole): The time-reversed version of a black hole, from which objects can only escape.

The Singularity and the Einstein-Rosen Bridge

The singularity (r=0) is a spacelike boundary. It's a moment in the future, not a place in space. The connection between Region I and Region III is an Einstein-Rosen Bridge, or wormhole. It is non-traversable because it collapses faster than any signal can cross it.
