The Physics of Reheating

Part A: The Inflaton Field

Inflation, the theory of exponential expansion in the very early universe, is driven by a hypothetical scalar field called the "inflaton" (φ). The key idea is that the energy density of the universe was dominated by the *potential energy* V(φ) of this field. If the potential is very flat, the field "slow-rolls" down it, behaving like a cosmological constant and causing spacetime to expand exponentially. This simulation lets you design V(φ) and see how its shape dictates the universe's evolution.

Part B: The Graceful Exit Problem

A major challenge for early models of inflation was how to end it. If inflation goes on forever, it doesn't create the universe we see. The "graceful exit" problem is the challenge of getting the inflaton to stop slow-rolling and settle into a minimum value, converting its energy into particles. Modern potentials, like the ones you can design here, solve this by having a "waterfall" region where the potential becomes very steep, causing the inflaton to accelerate, end the slow-roll phase, and begin oscillating around its minimum.

Part C: Reheating Dynamics

After inflation, the universe is vast, cold, and empty, with all its energy locked in the oscillating inflaton field. Reheating is the process of converting this energy into the hot soup of Standard Model particles we associate with the Big Bang. In the simplest models (perturbative reheating), the inflaton's oscillations decay into other particles, much like a radioactive atom decays. The strength of this decay is governed by the coupling constant Γ, which you can control. A larger Γ leads to faster, more efficient reheating and a higher final "reheat temperature."
